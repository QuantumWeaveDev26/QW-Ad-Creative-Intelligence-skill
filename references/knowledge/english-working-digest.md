## T01 — Creative essentials and psychology

Original: [T01](originals/01%20-%20Ad%20Creative%20Essentials%20and%20Psychology.txt); transcript starts at line 78.

Use one audience, one core message and one primary action. Separate hook (attention), body (trust, desire, objections), and CTA (action). Align with customer awareness.

14 hook families: question, pattern interrupt, contrarian statement, callout, stop-doing-this, transformation tease, statistic, story opening, comparison, visual shock, POV, direct claim, third-party validation and countdown/listicle. Nine body families: explanation, evidence, story, comparison, steps, objection handling, problem deep dive, social-proof stack and education. CTA families include purchase, low commitment, urgency, curiosity, community, question, risk reversal and next step.

Checked passage [11:13]: Separate hook, body and CTA so the creative has a clear structure. (English paraphrase).

## T02 — Angles and UGC

Original: [T02](originals/02%20-%20Ad%20Angles%20and%20UGC%20Strategies.txt); transcript starts at line 49.

An angle is the reason to care, distinct from hook and format. Expand audience problems, feature-to-value, objections, emotional outcomes and competitor gaps.

UGC is a native first-person presentation style, not necessarily influencer content. Structure: hook, problem, discovery, proof, objection answer, CTA. General formula: hook, problem, solution, credibility, pitch, CTA. Audience pools are messaging categories, not mandatory separate ad sets.

Checked passage [09:22]: Write three problems for each audience pool. (English paraphrase).

## T05 — Street interview and narrative formats

Original: [T05](originals/05%20-%20Street%20Interview%20Ad%20Strategies.txt); transcript starts at line 68.

Select a format that demonstrates a real audience insight. Separate single-narrator storytelling from multi-character drama.

Street: question, reaction montage, reveal, reaction, CTA. Drama: conflict, stakes, turning point, product resolution, CTA. Callout: specific audience/problem question, answer, follow-up, solution, CTA. POV: hook, hands-on action, CTA. Office: work, people and process. Review: encounter ad, test, verdict, suitability, CTA. AMA: question, answer, product, evidence, CTA. Story: relatable opening, event, resolution, subtle action. Suggestions to fake customers are not permission to deceive.

Checked passage [75:00]: Storytelling can use personal stories; mini drama uses multiple characters and a scripted scene. (English paraphrase).

## T07 — Script frameworks

Original: [T07](originals/07%20-%20Ad%20Script%20Writing%20Frameworks.txt); transcript starts at line 40.

Write one idea in spoken language, open strongly, keep necessary material and rehearse aloud.

HPSCPC: hook, problem, solution, credibility, pitch, CTA. Alternatives: before-change-after, myth busting, direct demo, proof stack and curiosity reveal. Choose for awareness and available evidence. Suggested durations are starting hypotheses, not platform requirements. The T-shirt/WhatsApp flow is an example, not a mandate to build messaging automation.

Checked passage [07:38]: Build introduction, credibility and proof in the body; begin with a real audience problem. (English paraphrase).
