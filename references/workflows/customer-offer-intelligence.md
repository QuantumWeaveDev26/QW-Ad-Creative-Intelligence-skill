# Ideal customers, buying motivations and offers
Operational extension applying T01 psychology/awareness, T02 audience/angle expansion and T03 customer value. Read those original passages before attribution.

1. Match research to the decision. Start with the supplied business facts, buyer's job, deliverables, exclusions and evidence. Research economics, region, capacity, customer/sales evidence or current competitors when those facts affect the requested recommendation. Cite current web evidence. A short fictional copy brief can use supplied facts and labelled hypotheses without competitor research. Resolve decision-blocking unknowns; do not fill missing operational facts with promises.
2. Identify ideal customer segments by problem, buying context and economics, not demographic guesses alone. Include job-to-be-done, trigger, urgency, desired outcome, alternative used, ability/willingness to pay, location, use case, qualification criteria and who should be excluded. For B2B distinguish user, influencer, decision maker, payer and procurement constraints. Rank fit, demand evidence, reachability and likely commercial value; do not infer sensitive traits.
3. Build a motivation table: practical gain, emotional driver, social driver, pain of inaction, switching barrier, objection, purchase trigger, evidence/quote and confidence. Label inferred motivations as hypotheses. Turn each priority insight into a distinct angle, not just another headline.
4. Draft offers around a specific outcome and actual deliverable. Specify audience, mechanism, inclusions, price/terms, meaningful value, proof, objections addressed, truthful risk reduction, one next step, fulfillment limits and economics. Use bonuses/guarantees/urgency only when verified and deliverable. Never invent an anchor price or imply a sample offer already exists.
5. Provide a compact segment shortlist, motivation map and a small set of differentiated offers. Recommend one starting offer and test hypothesis, with rationale, required proof, qualification questions, KPI and next action. High conversion is a test objective, not a guarantee.


For unfamiliar services, distinguish what the business offers from what customers hope it offers. A maintenance visit does not imply emergency response, certification or a repair guarantee. Ask qualification questions that change suitability or scope; do not turn every unknown into a required intake field.

For physical originals or customer property (such as family negatives), identify relevant handling, custody, return and file-access questions before promising an end-to-end service. Missing procedures are business decisions to resolve, not policies to invent. For technical replacement parts, dimensions and colour do not establish material, equipment compatibility, certification or food-contact suitability.
