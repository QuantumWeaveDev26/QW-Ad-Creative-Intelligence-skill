# Examples of meaningful ad image briefs
These are fictional structures, not ready-to-publish client claims. Replace all offer/product facts with verified inputs. Read image-generation.md for execution and visual-review.md for QA. Do not generate these merely because this reference is opened.

## Bulk team apparel: approval uncertainty
Buyer: HR/admin planning a 50+ person offsite. Angle: review logo placement before approving production, only if that is an actual service. Visual: the real garment beside a genuine placement preview and the relevant approval detail, with one concise headline. Preserve supplied fabric/print/logo. Do not invent customer messages or a quality guarantee. This process is not unique merely because the ad features it. A shirt on a podium with the word premium does not demonstrate this angle.

## SaaS workflow: fewer manual handoffs
Buyer: the operator performing a documented repetitive task. Visual: show a genuine authorized product step or a clearly labelled conceptual illustration of the workflow. Use measured time savings only if supported; otherwise show the mechanism without a numeric promise. Do not fabricate revenue dashboards. Preserve actual UI meaning and redact private data.

## Local service: process transparency
Buyer: someone unsure what the service includes. Visual: an authorized real process/detail and a clear inclusion/next step. Show what happens, not an invented five-star badge. Price, location, eligibility and timing must come from the business. Avoid unsupported before/after outcomes.

## Product use: one concrete benefit
Buyer: a specific person in a relevant use moment. Visual: the real product doing its ordinary, verified function in that setting. Focus copy on one benefit and action. Match packaging and physical behavior; do not add awards, ingredient labels, dramatic results or celebrity likenesses not supplied.
